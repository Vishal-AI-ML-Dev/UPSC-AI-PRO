# Resources and Their Conservation (synthetic NCERT-style chapter for evaluation)

Anything that can be used to satisfy a human need is called a resource. Water, soil,
minerals and forests become resources only when people find a use for them. Time and
technology can change whether a substance is a resource: crude oil was of little value until
engines were invented.

Resources are often classified by origin. Biotic resources come from living things, such as
forests, fish and livestock. Abiotic resources come from non-living things, such as rocks,
metals, sunlight and water.

---page---

Resources are also classified by how quickly they can be replaced. Renewable resources, such
as solar energy and wind energy, are replenished naturally in a short time. Non-renewable
resources, such as coal, petroleum and natural gas, take millions of years to form, so their
stock falls every time they are used.

Another classification is by the level of development. Potential resources are known to exist
but are not yet used, for example the strong winds along some coasts before wind farms are
built. Actual resources have been surveyed and their quantity and quality measured.

---page---

Land is a basic resource. Its use depends on physical factors such as soil, climate,
topography and the availability of water, and on human factors such as population and
technology. Land degradation is caused by deforestation, overgrazing, mining and the
excessive use of chemical fertilisers.

Soil conservation methods include mulching, contour ploughing, terrace farming, shelter belts
and strip cropping. In contour ploughing, fields are ploughed along the contours of a slope so
that the furrows slow down the flow of water and reduce soil erosion.

---page---

Sustainable development means meeting the needs of the present without compromising the
ability of future generations to meet their own needs. The idea asks people to use resources
carefully, to reduce waste, to reuse and recycle materials, and to protect biodiversity.

Rainwater harvesting is the collection and storage of rainwater for later use. Rooftop
rainwater harvesting directs water from roofs into tanks or into the ground to recharge
groundwater. It is one simple way in which households can conserve water.
